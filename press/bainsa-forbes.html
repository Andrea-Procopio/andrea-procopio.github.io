BAINSA, the AI research lab run by students

English summaryOriginal (Italian)

Lorenzo Caputi and Andrea Procopio discuss how BAINSA gives undergraduate students access to AI research, mentorship, and a community that supports their work.

Original interview by Francesco Innocente. Summarized in English from the Italian article.

The lab

BAINSA stands for Board of AI & NeuroScience Associations. It is Italy’s first and largest nonprofit AI research lab run by university students. Members work with academic and industry partners on papers supervised by experienced researchers and published at leading international conferences.

Alongside research, BAINSA organizes lectures, competitions, and hackathons, and funds conference travel. It brings together students selected for their preparation and motivation, with an emphasis on sharing what they learn and giving others access to the opportunities they have received.

How it began

BAINSA grew out of a gap in Italian universities: few opportunities for undergraduates to participate in research, especially in their first year. The founders wanted students to have access to opportunities comparable to those at leading American universities, where early involvement in research is already established.

The founders

Lorenzo and Andrea met while studying Economics and Computer Science at Bocconi and started BAINSA with other students there. Andrea’s research at Harvard led to a thesis developed with Lorenzo. When interviewed, Lorenzo was preparing to move to Stanford for a master’s in Statistics, while Andrea was beginning a PhD at MIT focused on AI.

Research and collaborations

Projects involve institutions and companies such as Harvard, ETH, Cambridge, and Bending Spoons, whose researchers mentor BAINSA students. One robotics team built a robotic arm that responds to brain activity measured through electroencephalography, or EEG.

The founders also point to Lead The Future, an Italian nonprofit, as an association that shares their commitment to merit, mentorship, and giving back.

What comes next

For the founders, research gives students a way to develop skills and experience early in their degrees. They want that technical development to go hand in hand with a sense of responsibility for how AI is developed and used.

The longer-term ambition is a network of student-run BAINSA branches at universities around the world. Through research, entrepreneurship, and support between members, they hope to help develop people and technology that benefit society.
