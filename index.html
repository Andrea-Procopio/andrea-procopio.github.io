Research Career

I'm 21, and I'm a first year PhD student at MIT with Prof. Dimitris Bertsimas, and a Research Fellow at Harvard with Prof. Tomer Ullman.

My interest revolves around AI systems for open-ended scientific discovery that can continually learn and adapt from experience, as we humans do.

Previously, I studied CS and Economics at Bocconi University and spent my final year doing research at Harvard on computer vision and cognitive science. I also co-founded BAINSA, Italy’s largest undergraduate-run AI research lab, and EuroTech Federation, a community of young founders in Europe.

01 / RESEARCH

My research thesis.

What makes a fifth-year PhD student produce better research than the first-year version of themselves—in quality, success rate, and time to results? Or what enables a seasoned artisan to create more intricate and tasteful work than their younger self?

I think the answer lies in extracting new, relevant knowledge by consolidating experience: what worked, what didn't, which kinds of reasoning and experimental directions turned out to be promising, and why. My hypothesis is that the process of discovery itself can be modeled, and that its underlying principles are domain-agnostic.

02 / MOTIVATION

What motivates me?

Historically, differences in social cooperation and technology have helped shape the trajectories of hominin species and human civilizations. I'm particularly excited about the latter: the possibility of making the process of scientific discovery cheaper, faster, and applicable across domains. I see this as the most effective way to expand human capabilities and raise the median quality of life.

It's also just really fun and cool.
